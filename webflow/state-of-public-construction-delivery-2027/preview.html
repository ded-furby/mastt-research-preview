Texas figures describe Texas DOT work only

Texas DOT builds roads and bridges under one set of specifications. Its results are a benchmark for that kind of work, not a measure of construction as a whole. The US federal and Australian figures cover many agencies and building types, and each country records change in a different way. Dollar figures for Texas DOT and US federal work are in US dollars. Commonwealth figures are in Australian dollars, and no figure is converted.

  • Days allowed are the contract days in the bid plus any days added by change order. Days charged are the working days counted against the contract.
  • A late penalty is liquidated damages charged for days over the allowed time. Every penalty equals days over multiplied by the daily rate.
  • Net change is final cost against the awarded amount. Gross change adds every movement in either direction.
  • For US federal and Commonwealth contracts, changed in value means at least one recorded modification or amendment that altered contract value.

17% of 10,041 completed Texas DOT construction jobs were charged exactly the days they were allowed. 61% were charged fewer days and 22% more, and only 1% finished one day late.

22% of Texas DOT jobs were charged late penalties, and the typical penalty was $18,000, or 0.7% of contract value. Texas DOT describes the charge as cost recovery, not a penalty, and sets the daily rate from the bid value of the contract.

78.5% of US federal construction contracts over $1m changed in value after signing. 73% of Commonwealth contracts of $7.5m or more were amended, and Texas DOT jobs showed a 5% net change that hid 13% gross movement.

Yes. 71% of US federal cost-plus construction contracts changed in value, against 39% of firm fixed-price contracts. The top tenth of cost-plus contracts grew by more than 285%.

Bigger jobs and civil work. 79% of Texas DOT jobs over $20m received extra days against 8% under $100k, and 29% of US federal heavy civil contracts had a change order against 8% for finishing trades.
