Only 59 first jobs fall in the over $5 million band, so that comparison is the least certain.

A price-history model missed the winning bid by a median 16.7% on 1,585 construction lettings in 2023-24, against 12.8% for the engineer's estimate. It was closer on 38% of lettings, and a 50/50 blend roughly matched the engineer.

A simple model trained on earlier Texas DOT contracts ranked a late job above an on-time job 79% of the time on contracts awarded from 2022. Its riskiest fifth ran late 23% of the time, against 4.5% for the rest, so it ranks risk without being a verdict on any one job.

It means a randomly chosen late job gets a higher risk score than a randomly chosen on-time job 79% of the time. A coin flip scores 0.5 and a perfect model scores 1.0.

Adding local climate averages moved the score from 0.780 to 0.782, which is inside the margin of error. Weather that actually happened lifts it more, to 0.785 or 0.802, but it is not known at bid time.

A simple classifier reached 94% accuracy across 24 document types, but its balanced score was only 0.74. Final payment claims scored 0.24, change proposals 0.33 and progress claims 0.40.

Only 19 of 103,102 public contract documents are RFIs, and 72% of the documents come from one owner. Owners publish awards, payments and completion records, while the working records stay inside projects.

At the halfway mark, compare time used with money earned. Texas DOT jobs more than 10 points behind paid late penalties on 47% of jobs, against 10% for jobs ahead.
